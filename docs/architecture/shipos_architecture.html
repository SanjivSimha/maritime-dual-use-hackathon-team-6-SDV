2 · What is being governed

Eight finite budgets, all checked on every decision

Hard breaking it means no or yes, if…    Elastic breaking it means yes, with a stated loss of capability
Hard

Power

Total and per line. Only 100 kW for guests with one generator down.

Hard

Heat

Every kW used becomes 1 kW of heat. Cooling falls as the sea warms: 120 kW at 30 °C.

Hard

Deck space

Bay 1 (whole or two halves), Bay 2, hangar, ramp. Bays can clash.

Hard

Mast

3 slots, 60 kg each. Scarce, and high weight counts triple for stability.

Hard

Mass & stability

10 t mission margin; 12 t stability-weighted budget.

Elastic

Data links

SATCOM 2 Mbit/s at 70% availability; zero under radio silence.

Mixed

Compute

4 nodes, 80% allocatable. CPU share is elastic; accelerators are whole units (3 usable).

Hard

Response time

Admission ≤ 30 s · right-of-way ≤ 2 s · first cut ≤ 5 s · safety ≤ 200 ms.

Why it matters Power is not the only limit. In warm water, cooling can run out before power does.

3 · The core idea

Four questions, one physics engine

All four decisions ask the same "does it fit?" check. They differ only in what options they try and what they optimise. The physics lives in one place, so the answers never contradict each other.

D1 · Admission

≤ 30 s

"Can this new equipment come aboard?" Tries removing, throttling or interlocking others. Answers YES YES-IF NOT NOW NO

D2 · Placement

part of D1

"Where should it go?" Tries every bay, slot and power line. Keeps the most headroom and future flexibility, and lists the top rejected layouts.

Shared
Feasibility kernel
For a given loadout, check all 8 budgets in every worst-case condition and return the tightest one
engine/kernel.py

D3 · Right of way

≤ 2 s

"Two guests need conflicting ship states. Who wins?" Drops unsafe options, then maximises mission value. States the cost to the loser.

D4 · Degradation

≤ 5 s / 60 s

"A generator tripped. What do we cut?" Throttle before standby, standby before shutdown. Never cuts safety-critical equipment or the D3 winner.

D5 Crew policy gate: confirms or blocks actions by crew size (14 / 6 / 0)
D6 Physical Account: every number, its source and where it is used
Design choice Change one number on a card and every decision updates consistently.

4 · How a decision is made

Six steps from request to recorded answer

The same pipeline runs for every decision. Nothing is learned, guessed or scripted.
1

Read inputs

Ship facts, equipment cards, mission priorities, operating envelope. All plain YAML files.

2

Check every budget

All 8 budgets, in every worst case the mission allows (see next slide).

3

Find the binding limit

The single budget with the least headroom. This becomes the reason.

4

Try every fix

Remove, throttle, defer or interlock others. The space is small, so we try them all.

5

Rank by mission

Cheapest loss of mission value wins. Weights come from the swappable priority file.

6

Explain & record

A sentence built from the binding numbers, appended to a tamper-evident log.

Why try every option?
5 guests × up to 4 modes ≈ 1,000 combinations. That takes milliseconds, gives the same answer every time, and produces the ranked list of alternatives the brief asks for.
Why "binding limit"?
Judges and crew don't need 40 numbers. They need the one that decides, with who is using it.
How does it scale?
A built-in guard switches to a greedy search if the space grows. A constraint solver (OR-Tools) is the next step.
Result Every answer comes with its reason, its alternatives and the cost to whoever loses.

10 · Data flow at runtime

One tick of the system

Every second the simulator builds an immutable snapshot of the world. If an event needs a decision, the pure engine answers and the log records it.
Why pure? Same inputs and seed always give the same decisions, so any run can be replayed and audited.

11 · Why you can trust the answers

Six guarantees, each enforced by an automated check

These aren't promises in a README. CI blocks any change that breaks them.
1

No magic numbers

Engine code may not contain numbers. Each one lives in a registry with its value, unit, range, reason and source.

✓ tests/test_no_literals.py
2

No canned explanations

Every rationale is assembled from the binding constraint. A test fails if a sentence lacks its numbers.

✓ tests/engine/test_rationale.py
3

Deterministic

No randomness or wall clock inside the engine. Same seed and timeline → byte-identical log hash.

✓ tests/sim/test_runner.py
4

Checked against the brief

Expected results are computed from Annex inputs inside the tests, never copied from engine output.

✓ tests/physics · tests/engine
5

Tamper-evident

The decision log is hash-chained. Replay re-runs the decisions, not just the hashes.

✓ shipos/sim/log.py verify_jsonl
6

Fails closed

Bad or ambiguous input is rejected as a whole. Unsupported actions stay blocked rather than guessed.

✓ tests/api/test_import_card.py
🔍 We checked the brief's arithmetic: "any four guests exceed 10 t" is false ({G1,G3,G4,G5} = 9.61 t) 🛡 Civil use only · no ML in decisions
Bottom line Every decision can be traced to a number, every number to a source.
